The Surveyor

The Surveyor finds the ores in the ground around it, and the old world's caches: chests and barrels it filled that nobody has opened since. It reads the ground a row of sixteen blocks at a time, a letter for each block, and asks its model where the first of each ore or cache in the row lies, just as the Survey test does. Every answer is checked against the block: a right one is kept, and the row is asked again for the next of that kind; a wrong one leaves that kind unfound until the next survey.

How it reads the ground

Its model is a language model, so the ground reaches it as text: each block a letter, a row of sixteen blocks sixteen letters. Ground: S stone, D deepslate, T other rock, A air, W water, L lava, B bedrock. Ores: C coal, I iron, U copper, O gold, R redstone, Z lapis, X diamond, E emerald; and K, a cache. After the row come >, the kind asked for and :, and the model answers with the first such block's place, a to p, or - for none: SSSTTIIDDDDSSSSS>I:f.

Surveying

Put a model in the Surveyor's Model slot, in the panel beside its screen: a Checkpoint, or a Relay Card tuned to a Model Server. It surveys 16 blocks each way and from 15 above it to 48 below: 4,096 rows, 32 read a tick and 64 questions put to the model at once. Then it starts over, so what is mined drops out of the next survey.

Its screen shows the eight ores and caches, three by three, with how many of each it found; along the screen's foot, how far the survey has gone. Hold a Survey Rod within 48 blocks of it to see what it found through the ground, a box in each ore's colour and caches in violet.

Digging for it

An agent's mine goes to what a Surveyor within 48 blocks found when nothing of the kind lies within its own reach of 8, and digs its way to anything buried: down where the ore lies at least as far below as aside, otherwise along the longer way, two blocks high. It tunnels only through rock and earth, never through a machine, a container or anything an axe would cut, and goes round such a block the other way when it can; it gives up a tunnel that would let in water or lava, or that finds no way round. To mine iron is to mine iron ore: repeat (mine 8 iron, put) digs where the iron is and brings it home. mine caches digs to a cache and takes what it holds.

Agents mining near each other work as a crew: each takes a block no other has picked, so a crew spreads over what a Surveyor found instead of crowding the nearest ore.

Its model

A model learns this on the Survey Dataset, and passes the Survey test by answering 90% of its questions right: see Models and Tests. Finding the first of a letter takes looking back over the row and knowing where each block stands in it. A model that sees only the last few characters, or a single layer of attention without positions, stays near half; a two-block transformer passes after about 1,000 steps. A model that reads ahead finds nothing, so the Surveyor refuses it.
