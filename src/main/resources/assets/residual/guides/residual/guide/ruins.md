# Ruins

The people who lived here before left their places behind: towers along the old roads, farms on the ridges, houses on
the marshes, slips on the shores, and stranger halls. Every kind of ruin is rare, some rarer than others, and each
stands only in the land that suits it, where the ground is near level: it takes travel to come across one, and a
Wayfinder to find one on purpose. Places of a kind were built a few different ways, and the years have worn each its
own way.

## Finding them

<residual:Clip name="wayfinder" alt="A Wayfinder tuned to a kind of place: its needle spins while it looks, then points" />

A <ItemLink id="wayfinder" /> leads to ruins the archive has told you of. Use it to choose a kind to look for: it lists
the relay towers, which everyone knows of, and every kind a shard you have read tells of; read a shard, by using it,
and the places it writes about join the list. Its needle spins while it looks, longer the farther the nearest one
lies, then points to the nearest ruin of that kind within 10,000 blocks; while you hold it, it says how far that is.

## Relay towers

Stone towers, the tallest thing for half a day's walk: a base buttressed at the corners, the shaft set back above it
and banded at each floor, and three rooms one over another, the store, the kitchen with its stove and the keeper's
room, with a ladder up the north wall. At the top a walk on corbels, railed, with the bell that calls for coal, goes
round the lamp room of glass in a copper frame, its great lamp of glass and redstone under a flared roof of copper gone
green. At the tower's foot stands the relay house: stone below and a spruce frame above, the keeper's desk with the
tower book on its lectern, and the stand where a big mind stood, ringed by accelerators in their cooling water. Some
towers have lost their tops. In grassland, forest, taiga, snow and hills, at most one in each region 48 chunks across.

## Waymarks

Stone pillars on stepped bases by stretches of the old road, a figure cut in the middle stone, a torch on top, little
stones left at the foot and an arrow to the nearest inn; the ring where walkers made their fire, with a bench and a
trough. Some pillars have fallen into the grass; one in six stands under a little roof with notes left on it. The
commonest ruin: at most one in each region 24 chunks across.

## Three Wells

A market town's square where two roads cross like an X: paving round three wells under their little roofs, a lamp at
every corner, the bell at the crossing, the notice board with the market rules, stalls under wool awnings, benches
under two trees, the bakery with its oven built out on the side, the school house by the east well with its painted
door and its bell on the roof, and a stretch of the town wall. On plains, meadows and savannas, the rarest of the
towns' ruins: at most one in each region 80 chunks across.

## Inns

The inns of the Long Road, a day apart, the Halfway Inn and the Brown Hen: a timber frame on a stone footing (the Brown
Hen's ground floor all stone), the upper floor jutting over the road on corbels, shuttered windows, two dormers and the
chimney stack up the gable. Inside, the hearth still burns; the boards hang by the fire, the book lies behind the
counter next to a pie, and the beds wait upstairs; a woodshed stands at the back, the stable across the yard with its
roof part fallen, and the inn's sign by the road. At most one in each region 64 chunks across.

## Windmill farms

The farms of the ridges: a windmill with a stone base and a boarded body, a railed stage round it and its tally board by
the door, the barn with its hayloft and hoist, the pig pen and the hen house, a well in the yard, and three fields,
wheat, beetroot, and potatoes and carrots, each with its ditch, its hedge and its scarecrow or hives. On plains,
meadows, savannas and windswept hills, at most one in each region 56 chunks across.

## Watermills

The mills of the orchard country, of birch and glass on a stone footing at the edge of a pond: the wheel out over the
water on its axle, the millstone under its hopper inside, a race of stone beside it with the sluice nobody may open and
a footbridge with a loose plank, the notices on the gate, a bench on the far side, old apple trees and hives among the
flowers. Only by water deep enough, the wheel toward it: at most one in each region 56 chunks across.

## Stilt houses

Houses of mud brick on decks over the marsh, their stilts down to the marsh floor, a boardwalk to the bank. A healer's
house keeps its high shelves of labeled jars, the remedies on the bench, beds for the sick and her day book; a boat
house keeps the day's catch and the tally on its wall. In swamps and mangrove swamps, at most one in each region 48
chunks across.

## Slipways

A buttressed stone quay with steps down to the water, the shipwright's shed with its window for mending nets, the wood
store and the fish market's price board; running out over the sea on posts, the frame of a ship never finished with a
crane leaning over it, and beside it a ramp of stone into the water. Some frames are a big ship's, three times as long
as any boat. Only where a beach or a stony shore meets deep enough water, the slip toward the sea: at most one in each
region 40 chunks across.

## Tutor houses

Halls of tuff between pillars, two tiers of tall windows under a cornice of copper, a relay on the roof, where minds were
drilled and tested. Apprentices still stand in their row before the tutor's Bench; the teaching room keeps its tray of
crystals, each on its card, in the light of its bay window; the apprentice hall across the yard keeps its dry box; and
the drill yard, ringed with torches, has its boards drawn as small fields. In grassland, forest, taiga, savanna and
cherry groves, at most one in each region 72 chunks across.

## The Archive at Vell

Where everything was kept, dug into its hill. Its front faces the town: pillars of tuff, the portal under the first
keepers' words, a broad flight of steps, the copying room's big window and the reading room's, where a lamp still burns
on the long table; behind the front everything is under turf, and light wells of glass come up through the grass. A
great stair goes down into the hill to the front hall and the finding book, the halls of shelves under their light
wells, a stack sealed with clay, and the deepest room, where the oldest record is kept. At most one in each region 160
chunks across.

## The Deep Works

A great mine in the hills. At the shaft head stand the headframe and its wheel, the board where miners wrote their
names and rubbed them out coming up, the tool store and the sorting bins. A ladder goes down the shaft past a level of
copper, a level of iron and coal with an amethyst hollow behind a door, and the deep stone, where an old tunnel was
walled up; at the ninth level the gate is locked, and an old side door above it leads down another way. In windswept
hills, meadows and savanna plateaus, at most one in each region 128 chunks across.

## Fallen relay masts

Where a relay mast fell it broke a bowl in the ground, grassed over at the rim. The mast lies across it in three pieces
of green copper, its relay half buried at the far end, stone thrown up round it; along the rim the keepers put a rope
of chain on posts, and their sign. Its drive housing split open: the chest inside holds
<ItemLink id="archive_drive" />s already filled with the relays' writing. At most one in each region 40 chunks across.

## Sealed stacks

Deep under the ground, with no way in but a wall of clay: a vaulted hall of the archive sealed in its last days, its
shelves in bays behind folded doors with wax over them, boxes of records in waxed cloth down the middle, the sealing
book on its lectern, and a lamp left on the long table; roots have come down through the vault. Their boxes hold filled
<ItemLink id="archive_drive" />s. At most one in each region 64 chunks across.

## Archive houses, relay masts and buried vaults

Small houses of calcite between pillars of tuff under roofs of deepslate tiles, one corner usually fallen in, that kept
records; open masts of green copper on stepped tuff footings, an old relay still on top of most of them; and, under a
lantern on a post beside a hatch, deep rooms with a bank of old accelerators and two chests, every one holding a
[salvaged model](salvage.md). At most one in each region 56 chunks across, the vaults 64.

## What they hold

- <ItemLink id="archive_shard" />: pieces of the archive, mostly of what was written about the place they lie in: a
  relay tower's shards are mostly its keepers' writing. See [The Archive](archive.md).
- Photomasks with salvaged designs: the Fabricator makes working but unfused machines from them, without a Bench.
  Linear layer, softmax and GELU masks turn up in houses, matrix multiply ones under the gravel, and attention, layer
  norm, feed-forward and transformer masks in vaults. See [The Bench and the Fabricator](bench.md).
- <ItemLink id="schematic_relays" />: the plans for relays, a pattern to copy that stays in the crafting grid. In
  relay towers, on masts and in vaults.
- What each place kept: coal and food in the towers, flour and seed on the farms, remedies in the marsh, timber and
  tools at the slips; <ItemLink id="weight_crystal" />, blank <ItemLink id="checkpoint" />s and a little gold.
- <ItemLink id="salvaged_model" />: see [Salvaged Models](salvage.md).
