Machines and Cables

A model is a graph of operations, and you build that graph out of blocks. Values flow out of one machine and into the next through cables, from the Model Core's tokens to the scores it takes back.

Ports

Every machine computes one output from its inputs, and each face of the block has a job:

  • Front: the output.
  • Back: the main input.
  • Every other face: the side input, on machines that take two inputs, such as the Residual Junction or the Matrix Multiplier. One-input machines leave their sides unused, and the Position Table has no input at all.

The Model Core sends tokens out of its front and takes the finished scores in through any other face.

Look at a machine to see which port the face under your crosshair is. A machine placed against another machine's output takes that output as its input; placed against an input, it feeds it; placed against a cable or a residual stream, it reads it. Anywhere else its output points away from you. Sneak-use a face to turn the output to that face; sneak-using the output face itself turns the machine around.

Networks

Cables that touch form a network, and a network carries exactly one value. So every network needs exactly one source, a machine's front or the Core's front, and any number of inputs may read it. Two sources on one network is an error, and so is an input on a network with no source.

Colors

Colored cables only join cables of their own color, while plain cables join any color. That lets two networks run side by side without mixing. Dye eight cables at a time; a water bucket washes the color out again.

Tracing a value

With a Tensor Probe in hand, looking at a cable tells you which machine drives it and how many inputs read it. Use the probe on a cable to list every port on its network in chat.

The probe also shows every machine's output as a hologram above it, from the last time the model ran: look at one to read its values. See Training for what they show while a model learns.
