Residual

Someone lived here before you. They seem to have built machines that thought, and they wrote everything down. Then they were gone. Pieces of what they left still turn up in the ruins: broken tablets of their writing, the designs of their machines, and models that no longer work.

These are field notes on building models again: language models made of machines that each do one operation, cabled into a network, trained on text, and tested on text they have never seen. A model that knows what the blocks around it will do next lets a Forecaster run them ahead of time. Others carry on a pattern, tell what a thing is by its name, turn what you say into programs for agents, and read what the old world wrote.

Putting models to work

  • The Forecaster: a model of the blocks around it, trusted only as far as it is right, runs them ahead.
  • Agents: frames that run programs, with models where learning beats a rule.
  • The Architect: agents that copy, move and repeat builds, read as letters a slice at a time.
  • The Surveyor: a model that finds the ores in the ground around it, read a row at a time.
  • Serving Models: Model Servers, relays and the Terminal.

The base

  • Storage: drives that keep any number of kinds, a terminal that finds anything, and Ports that move items between the network and every chest and machine, 1,280 a second.
  • Power: generators, cables and cells, and a Smelter that makes two ingots from every ore, three with a controller.
  • Flight: the flight frame, flown by a pilot you train.
